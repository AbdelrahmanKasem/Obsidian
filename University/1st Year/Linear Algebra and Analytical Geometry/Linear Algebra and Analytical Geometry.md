# Module 1. Analytic Geometry

Lectures 1–7

This module introduces the mathematical tools used to describe and investigate geometric objects in the plane and in space.

## Lecture 1 — Vectors and Coordinate Systems

### Main Topics

Geometric vectors, Linear operations on vectors, Cartesian basis, Cartesian coordinates of a vector, Cartesian coordinate system, Cartesian coordinates of a point
### What to Understand

This lecture establishes the connection between geometric objects and their algebraic representation.

You should understand how to represent vectors and points using coordinates and how to perform basic operations with vectors.

### References

- L.1: pp. 44–63
    
- L.4: pp. 3–22
    

## Lecture 2 — Scalar and Vector Products

### Main Topics

Scalar product, Properties of the scalar product, Coordinate expression of the scalar product, Vector product, Properties of the vector product

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces two important operations on vectors.

You should understand how the scalar product is used to calculate angles and lengths, and how the vector product is used to construct a vector perpendicular to two given vectors.

### References

- L.1: pp. 63–76
    
- L.4: pp. 23–31
    

## Lecture 3 — Vector Product in Coordinate Form and Mixed Product

### Main Topics

Vector product in coordinate form, Mixed product, Geometric meaning of the mixed product, Coordinate expression of the mixed product

### What to Understand

This lecture develops the coordinate formulas for vector products and introduces the mixed product.

You should understand how the mixed product is connected to the volume of a parallelepiped and how it can be calculated using coordinates.

### References

- L.1: pp. 71–72, 76–80
    
- L.4: pp. 32–36
    

## Lecture 4 — Lines in the Plane

### Main Topics

General equation of a line, Canonical equation of a line, Parametric equation of a line, Equation of a line with slope, Angle between two lines, Normalized equation of a line, Distance from a point to a line

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the main forms of the equation of a line.

You should understand how to represent a line using different equations and how to calculate angles and distances involving lines.

### References

- L.1: pp. 118–129
    
- L.4: pp. 37–48
    

## Lecture 5 — Planes and Lines in Space

### Main Topics

General equation of a plane, Normalized equation of a plane, Distance from a point to a plane, Canonical equation of a line in space, Parametric equation of a line in space, A line as the intersection of two planes, Angles between two lines, Angle between a line and a plane

### What to Understand

This lecture extends the methods of analytic geometry from the plane to three-dimensional space.

You should understand how to describe lines and planes in space and how to investigate their relative positions using equations and vectors.

### References

- L.1: pp. 136–154
    
- L.4: pp. 49–59
    

## Lecture 6 — Second-Order Curves

### Main Topics

Derivation of the canonical equation of the ellipse, Derivation of the canonical equation of the hyperbola, Derivation of the canonical equation of the parabola

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the three main conic sections.

You should understand how their canonical equations are derived and how the equations describe the geometric properties of each curve.

### References

- L.1: pp. 155–168
    
- L.4: pp. 60–70
    

## Lecture 7 — Second-Order Surfaces and Coordinate Transformations

### Main Topics

Classification of second-order surfaces, Canonical equations of second-order surfaces, Transformation of Cartesian rectangular coordinates in the plane

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the classification of second-order surfaces and the role of coordinate transformations in simplifying geometric equations.

You should understand how different surfaces are identified from their canonical equations and how changing coordinates can make a geometric problem easier to analyze.

### References

- L.1: pp. 81–84, 109–111, 116–117, 210–220
    
- L.4: pp. 71–80
    

# Module 2. Linear Algebra

Lectures 8–16

This module develops the fundamental concepts of linear algebra, beginning with determinants and matrices and progressing to linear spaces, operators, and quadratic forms.

## Lecture 8 — Determinants and Matrices

### Main Topics

Definition of an n-th order determinant ✅, Properties of determinants, Matrix addition ✅, Scalar multiplication of matrices ✅, Matrix multiplication ✅, Inverse matrix, Construction of the inverse matrix using the adjugate matrix method

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the main algebraic tools used throughout linear algebra.

You should understand how to perform matrix operations, calculate determinants, and find the inverse of a matrix.

### References

- L.3: pp. 17–32, 93–98
    
- L.4: pp. 81–92, 93–98
    

## Lecture 9 — Rank of a Matrix

### Main Topics

Definition of matrix rank, Basis minor theorem, Methods for finding the rank of a matrix

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the concept of matrix rank, which is essential for studying systems of linear equations and linear dependence.

You should understand how to determine the rank of a matrix and how the basis minor theorem is used in this process.

### References

- L.2: pp. 39–45
    
- L.3: pp. 32–41
    
- L.4: pp. 98–106
    

## Lecture 10 — Systems of Linear Equations

### Main Topics

Gaussian elimination method, Cramer's rule

### What to Understand

This lecture focuses on solving systems of linear equations.

You should understand how to solve systems using Gaussian elimination and Cramer's rule, and when each method is appropriate.

### References

- L.2: pp. 75–79
    
- L.3: pp. 50–54
    
- L.4: pp. 107–116
    

## Lecture 11 — General Systems and Homogeneous Systems

### Main Topics

Kronecker–Capelli theorem, Investigation of arbitrary systems of linear algebraic equations, Homogeneous systems, Fundamental system of solutions

### What to Understand

This lecture develops the theoretical conditions for the existence and number of solutions of linear systems.

You should understand how to determine whether a system has no solution, one solution, or infinitely many solutions, and how homogeneous systems are investigated.

### References

- L.2: pp. 80–84, 31–45, 45–50
    
- L.4: pp. 116–123
    

## Lecture 12 — Linear Spaces, Part 1

### Main Topics

Definition of a linear space, Examples of linear spaces, Linear dependence and independence, Basis, Coordinates of a vector, Linear operations in coordinate form

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the abstract concept of a linear space.

You should understand what makes a set a linear space, how vectors can be expressed as linear combinations, and how basis vectors are used to represent other vectors.

### References

- L.2: pp. 51–55
    
- L.3: pp. 62–65
    
- L.4: pp. 124–133
    

## Lecture 13 — Linear Spaces, Part 2

### Main Topics

Dimension of a linear space, Relation between bases, Concept of a linear subspace

### What to Understand

This lecture develops the concepts of dimension and subspaces.

You should understand how different bases of the same space are related and how the dimension of a space is determined.

### References

- L.2: pp. 55–57, 58–62
    
- L.3: pp. 65–70, 73–78
    
- L.4: pp. 133–142
    

## Lecture 14 — Linear Operators

### Main Topics

Definition of a linear operator, Examples of linear operators, Matrix of a linear operator, Eigenvectors, Eigenvalues

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces linear transformations and their matrix representations.

You should understand how a linear operator acts on vectors and how eigenvalues and eigenvectors describe important properties of that operator.

### References

- L.3: pp. 92–98, 119–128
    
- L.4: pp. 143–155
    

## Lecture 15 — Euclidean Spaces and Gram–Schmidt Orthogonalization

### Main Topics

Definition of a Euclidean space, Examples of Euclidean spaces, Orthogonal systems, Orthonormal systems, Cauchy–Bunyakovsky inequality, Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization process

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces the geometric structure of Euclidean spaces.

You should understand the concepts of orthogonality and orthonormality, the Cauchy–Bunyakovsky inequality, and how the Gram–Schmidt process converts a linearly independent system into an orthogonal or orthonormal one.

### References

- L.2: pp. 90–103
    
- L.3: pp. 144–157, 173–177
    
- L.4: pp. 156–172
    

## Lecture 16 — Quadratic Forms

### Main Topics

Quadratic forms, Matrix representation of quadratic forms, Change of basis, Reduction to canonical form, Positive definiteness, Negative definiteness

### What to Understand

This lecture introduces quadratic forms and their matrix representation.

You should understand how quadratic forms change under a change of basis and how they can be reduced to canonical form.

### References

- L.3: pp. 187–198
    
- L.4: pp. 173–189
